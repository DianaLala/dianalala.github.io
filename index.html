    How I think

    Nothing gets designed until people have had their say

    Most of my projects follow these six steps. The photos are from real workshops and field visits.

    1. 1

      Understand people & context

      Transect walks, photo walks, interviews and focus groups in the places where the work will be used.

    2. 2

      Define the problem

      Empathy maps and personas turn what we heard into a challenge everyone agrees on.

    3. 3

      Co-create ideas

      Users, experts and partners in one room, generating ideas together so the result belongs to them.

    4. 4

      Prototype

      Quick paper versions of games, cards and pages that people can hold and react to.

    5. 5

      Test & refine

      Pre-tests with the real audience: is it clear, relevant and culturally right? Then revise.

    6. 6

      Deliver & learn

      Final production, training of trainers, and a report so the learning travels with the tools.

      About

      The creative thinker behind the work

      I'm a Kampala-based designer, illustrator and co-founder of Urithi Studio. For over 15 years I've worked where research meets the people it's meant to serve.

      I trained in Art and Industrial Design at Kyambogo University, then spent 11 years at Mango Tree turning co-creation sessions into prototypes and finished tools for international clients. At Light for the World I led innovation and documentation, making materials accessible to persons with disabilities. Today, at Urithi Studio, I lead social and behaviour change projects from first interview to final print.

      I co-create with the audience instead of designing at them: farmers, young people, health workers, persons with disabilities. Then I make the thing myself, whether that's a board game, a comic, a manual or a campaign.

      • I wrote a puppet play

        In 2017 I was one of nine illustrators behind 50 children's books for the Mango Tree Literacy Lab, and wrote a puppet theatre play to go with them.

      • My paintings move

        My collection Looking Within uses augmented reality, so the women in the paintings step out of their frames.

      • I've run co-creation in four countries

        Uganda, Zambia, Botswana and Mozambique, including a master class for farmers with disabilities in Beira.

      • I draw potatoes with opinions

        Pot-Tet-Oes is my personal comic strip.
